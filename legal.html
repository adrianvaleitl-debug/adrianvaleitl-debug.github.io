3. Cookies and browser storage

The website code reviewed for this notice does not set advertising or analytics cookies, tracking pixels, third-party advertising tags, external JavaScript SDKs or embedded third-party video frames. It offers an optional appearance preference feature. If you choose “Allow appearance storage” in the on-page privacy notice, your light/dark mode and accent colour are saved in browser local storage so they can be restored on a later visit. If you choose “Don’t save appearance”, previously saved appearance preferences across these project pages are cleared and new appearance choices are used only for the current page session. The project websites share one allow/decline choice across this GitHub Pages domain; that decision is stored so it can be respected on later visits, while appearance preferences are stored separately for each project page. These browser-storage values remain on your device and are not sent to the creator. These are browser-storage entries, not cookies.

Some pages make direct requests to GitHub for public release information or project-logo images; those requests may expose technical request data to GitHub. No advertising or analytics tracking is intentionally used. If new non-essential cookies or similar tracking storage are introduced, they will be disabled until a valid consent mechanism is in place. You can change your decision using the “Privacy choices” control on the website, or clear the site's browser storage through your browser's site-data settings; clearing it resets the saved appearance choice and privacy preference.

Children and age-appropriate use

These projects are general-purpose and are not specifically designed to collect children's personal information. The reviewed websites do not ask visitors to register, provide a date of birth or submit personal information through a form. The desktop apps store information locally on the user's device and do not create child accounts. No age-verification gate or parental-consent workflow is currently used. UK guidance says the Children's Code can apply to services likely to be accessed by under-18s, even when they are not aimed specifically at children. The ICO describes “likely” access as more probable than not, considering the nature and content of the service and evidence about real-world use. A documented assessment of whether each service meets that test has not yet been completed, so this notice is not a claim of full Children's Code compliance. The current design avoids online child accounts, targeted advertising, profiling and geolocation; planning and business records are stored locally. Before marketing to under-18s or adding cloud accounts, profiling, advertising or other online personal-data processing, the likely audience must be assessed and age-appropriate protections and any required parental-consent process implemented. Do not enter a child's personal details unless necessary and appropriate. If you believe a child has sent personal information to the creator, contact the email above.
